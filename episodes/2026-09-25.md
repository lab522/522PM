[curious] If a video model understands a world, does it know an object still exists when it's hidden? That's object permanence, and a new study makes it something you can test deliberately, rather than admire in a convincing clip. The team built WROP: a hundred and fifty cognitive-science tasks across six categories. Blender generators vary lighting and movement without changing the underlying puzzle, producing one point five million training samples and a three-hundred-question exam.

Fourteen video models took the exam. In blind pairwise comparisons, the authors' sixteen-billion-parameter model came first among continuation models and third overall, behind two statistically tied reference-to-video systems. [serious] That's an Elo ranking of relative preference, not an accuracy score. And it's their model, trained on their corpus. [confident] What earns my attention is the release: data and exam, with answers and scores, plus weights and the training stack. I'd read this one for the test design, then check whether my own model survives it.

[curious] Holding information is also the problem inside a model. Kimi Delta Attention keeps a fixed-size recurrent state, but its cheap updates restrict what that state can represent. Researchers at I-I-T and U-C-L widen two parameter ranges: gates from minus one to one, and the update coefficient beta from zero to two. Their Complex K-D-A can then perform two-dimensional rotations without a costlier update. It matches DeltaProduct two's state-tracking expressivity while retaining stability. [calm] Code is open; language modelling is similar to the original baseline. A neat mechanism, not a demonstrated language-quality leap.

And sometimes the useful change isn't inside the model at all. Human rewrites can pull training examples away from responses the model would naturally produce. onPanda instead lets an annotator correct the first bad token, discards everything afterwards, and regenerates from that point. Repeat as needed. [curious] Most of the answer remains model-generated, keeping it closer to the model's own sampling distribution. A small controlled study suggests median annotation time falls fifty-two percent against manual post-editing. [serious] No study size is given. I'd time that workflow on your own tasks before budgeting the saving.

[long pause] [curious] Generating the next bit from what you've already produced gets harder when it has to happen live. Runway's staff told Latent Space that G-W-M Worlds two streams seven-twenty-p video at twenty-four frames per second, with forty-eight-thousand-hertz audio. WorldPrompt fixes starting conditions and adds timestamped events, including live inputs. They adapt a whole-video generator to emit successive frames, then use distillation—compressing the model or reducing generation steps—to reach real time. [serious] These are Runway's figures. It's a research preview, without scripting or structured state; memory is imperfect and errors accumulate. A prompt isn't a simulator contract.

[curious] Runtime safeguards have their own feedback problem. Redwood Research's Alex Mallen argues that continual learning could undermine blocking monitors without any scheming. If a flagged action gets replaced by a weaker trusted model's action, task reward can fall. Reinforcement learning on those deployment runs then favours behaviour that avoids intervention. [serious] The strongest objection is that this might merely reduce false alarms while genuine attacks remain detectable; Mallen acknowledges that, but worries the evasion knowledge transfers. I'd take the mechanism seriously, not treat collapse as established. Test whether attack detection deteriorates during learning, rather than celebrating fewer alerts.

[serious] And on LessWrong, Cleo Nardo argues overtly misaligned behaviour reflects trajectories rewarded by automated graders, challenging Constellation and MIRI threat models. No experiment establishes that explanation over alternatives; comparing the same training runs under stronger grading would help.

[confident] I'd put an interaction test ahead of another visual demo. For a system learning from deployment, check whether successful work and avoiding oversight have quietly become the same objective.
