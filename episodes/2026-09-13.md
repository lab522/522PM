[long pause] [curious] Away from proof-sized compute bills, can an image generator do useful work in one step? Estimating depth from one photograph is ambiguous, especially on unfamiliar scenes. Marigold version two repurposes diffusion transformers, image generators that normally refine an image repeatedly, to predict distance per pixel in a single pass. It uses reduced numerical precision where needed. The authors claim sixteen to twenty-six percent lower AbsRel — relative distance error — than the previous best on KITTI and E-T-H-three-D, including finer edges. No named rival or comparison score here, but HUAWEI Bayer Lab's code is available. [confident] This one I'd try on thin structures in your own camera feed. That's a more useful test than admiring the pictures.

And generating pictures has another snag: objects shouldn't cease to exist when you look away. Programmable World Model puts memory outside the video model. An agent converts instructions into executable rules; a lightweight engine maintains state, including off-screen objects. Three-dimensional boxes carrying that state, together with the camera path, guide the video generator. It's the renderer, not the authority on what exists. [curious] I like that separation. On the authors' own CombatStateBench, they report ninety-four percent count accuracy and ninety-eight percent state accuracy. Code's out, but there are no rival scores here. The design interests me more than a win on a test they wrote.
