[curious] A repository doesn't need a lively issue tracker to contain useful work. That's the premise behind CodeMidas: training coding agents needs automatically gradable tasks, but harvesting issues and fixing commits limits what you can teach. Here, source code is the only task-specific input. Agents explore existing functionality and write behavioural specifications. They build tests against the original code, then filter tasks through execution checks and repeated attempts to solve them. [confident] That's the bit I'd inspect: whether the tests capture the behaviour, rather than just rewarding a plausible imitation. The pipeline produces five thousand five hundred and forty-five tasks from three thousand one hundred and eighty-five repositories, spanning twenty-three languages. Training one model with reinforcement learning, which rewards successful attempts, reportedly improves DeepSWE by eleven point seven percent and ProgramBench by seventeen percent. Terminal-Bench two point one gains eight point five percent. More high-quality tasks helped in controlled comparisons.

[serious] But those are changes without absolute starting scores, and only three of five benchmarks are named. One trained model, no replication. Promising task supply; not yet a dependable performance forecast.

[curious] And a plausible imitation is exactly what Qwen's RecreationWorld tries to catch. An agent gets a running application to explore, then must rebuild it without a prescribed workflow. The harness combines graphical control with coding across five platforms. Its held-out RecreationBench has two hundred and fifty tasks, with assertions checked against the reference application and by human reviewers. G-P-T-six Astra leads at fifty-eight point one percent overall, but passes every programmatic test on just two point eight percent of tasks. [serious] Static layouts come out better than interactions and computed results. I'd take that gap seriously: looking finished isn't being finished. The benchmark code is available; claimed training transfer has no named comparison or gain figure.

[curious] Working backwards from an existing result also turns up in chemistry, though here the goal is a recipe, not a replica. Retrosynthesis starts with a target molecule and works back towards simpler ingredients. Microsoft Research says its Nature-published RetroChimera combines a transformer that generates precursor molecules directly with a graph network that applies reaction templates. Learned votes rank their suggestions, adding support when both agree. [confident] Complementary weaknesses, rather than two copies of the same guess: that's the attraction. Microsoft says expert assessment found success on nine of ten challenging multistep targets, against five and four for the component models; another baseline managed two. Ten targets is small, and that's the lab's account. But implementation and weights are out under the M-I-T licence.

[curious] Keeping outputs intact gets harder when you shrink the model itself: Multiverse Computing treats block removal as a physics-style optimisation, accounting for how removal decisions interact, and claims almost twenty-three percentage points over the best competing block-removal method on the M-M-L-U knowledge benchmark at fifty percent compression of a large Llama instruct model. Its cheap scoring proxy isn't perfect. And compression can blur robot movements too: ActionPiece trains tokenisation to preserve which actions are physically closer to one another, reporting ninety-four point eight percent on LIBERO, but gives no competing tokenizer score.

[long pause] [serious] Beyond how models work, there's who controls their distribution. In prepared Congressional remarks, Nathan Lambert cites Chinese open-weight models' three point two billion Hugging Face downloads, roughly twice America's, arguing that restricting open software is impractical and ecosystem preparation matters more; download counts alone don't establish dependence, so I'd want evidence of actual deployment before drawing that conclusion. [confident] For something you can test tomorrow, benchmark the tokenizer on your own traffic. For the agent work, inspect a generated test before committing to a training run.
