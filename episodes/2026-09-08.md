[curious] Iris makes search questions harder to bluff. Ziyuan Liu and colleagues build multi-hop tasks from web hyperlinks, replacing non-answer entities with descriptions to defeat string matching. A reference model must fail closed-book but succeed with evidence. Filtered trajectories then train agents with thirty-five billion parameters, three billion active, and three hundred and ninety-seven billion, seventeen billion active. [confident] Code's available; no benchmark numbers here. I'd inspect that filtering pipeline first.

[short pause] But finding evidence isn't the same as coordinating agents. Bilevel Coordinated Reflection models orchestrators and workers as a game. Under bounded coupling, decomposition quality controls equilibrium slack. It also derives finite-time bounds for free-form reflection, including a positive lower bound under a persistent-harm condition. [serious] Conditional theory, not a guarantee that another reflection pass helps.

Now, a more physical test: Nima Mohajerin and Steven Waslander add feedforward inter-layer connections to recurrent networks. Their quadrotor altitude case study shows better dynamics learning and generalisation. Useful evidence, but one case study.

[curious] From controlling motion to generating it: Princeton's UniMate takes a rigged three-dimensional asset and a text prompt, producing motion across skeletons without per-skeleton retraining or test-time optimisation. Code's available. That's the practical claim to test.

[short pause] Meanwhile, discrete diffusion offers a different route around sequential decoding. Diffusion-augmented language models separate next-token-trained autoregressive weights from lightweight diffusion weights trained by distillation. Psi-Spec samplers claim lossless parallel sampling. [confident] Lossless means preserving the distribution; it doesn't give you a speedup number.

And closer to the hardware, Google's MaxKernel uses compiler feedback and specialist agents for planning, debugging, testing and profiling T-P-U kernels. It offers human-guided development, autonomous metric-driven optimisation and graph-based search. Code's available; compare against your tuned kernels.

[curious] Back in three dimensions, WorldSculpt adapts a single-object generative prior to multiple views, generating cluttered scenes with hundreds of separate object meshes. Unlike a single fused reconstruction, you get objects in a shared frame. I'd check the occluded geometry.

Which brings us back to checking your own work. Tencent Hunyuan's FlowBalance scores on-policy trajectories using token-level probability gains from a frozen view of the policy with privileged context. Verifier-derived advantage keeps that guidance for positive trajectories, reverses it for negative ones and disables it without an outcome preference. [confident] A neat safeguard against self-approval.

[short pause] Staying with training: Salesforce's RISE explores recursive improvement through self-extrapolating policy distillation; Verify Before You Distill proposes prompt-level teacher gating. Don't Drop Dropout tackles layer sparsity for training and inference. And Ask Before You Optimize puts clarification before interactive optimisation's formulation.

On behaviour, Beneath the Surface of Chains-of-Thought examines reasoning operations mechanistically. Refuse without Refusal analyses safety-tuning responses to reduce false refusals. And When Models Edit Too Much studies minimal code-edit fidelity. [curious] That last one is worth checking if your patch agent keeps redecorating the house.

[short pause] Now, the argument over Astra. Don't Worry About the Vase challenges the combination of high capability, poor monitorability and OpenAI's alignment claims. Its other discussion centres on Jakub Pachocki, who argues that smarter models are needed to defend against other A-I. [serious] The strongest case is defence keeping pace with offence. My objection: greater capability alone doesn't establish dependable defence, especially if monitoring gets harder. What would settle it? Controlled defensive evaluations, with monitor failures measured rather than waved away.

[confident] I'd try Iris's task filter on your own search failures, then check whether FlowBalance's sign reversal survives an ablation. Keep an eye on reproducible monitorability evidence for Astra.
